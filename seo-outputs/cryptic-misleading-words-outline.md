## FAQ drafts (FAQPage schema — required)

**Q: Why does "flower" mean river in a cryptic crossword?**
A: Because cryptic setters read *flower* as *flow + er* — "a thing that flows" — which is a river, not a plant. This is an agent-noun trick: any verb plus "-er" can become a misleading noun. It's a recognised convention, so dictionaries won't list this meaning, but solvers are expected to know it.

**Q: What does "number" mean in a cryptic crossword?**
A: *Number* usually means an anaesthetic, painkiller, or something cold — anything that "numbs" (numb + er = "one that numbs"). It rarely refers to a digit. The capitalised or sentence-start position is a decoy to push you toward the maths meaning.

**Q: What does "banker" mean in cryptic crosswords?**
A: *Banker* most often means a river — a thing that *has banks* — not a financier. Like flower, it's a misleading agent noun. Setters rely on you picturing money first. Other river-words include *flower* and sometimes *runner*.

**Q: Are these misleading meanings considered fair?**
A: Yes. These "invented" agent-noun and double-definition meanings are a long-standing, accepted part of cryptic convention. A clue is fair as long as the wordplay genuinely leads to the answer — even if the surface reading deliberately misleads you.

**Q: How do I get better at spotting cryptic misdirection?**
A: Learn the common families first (the "-er" agent nouns and the "usual suspects" abbreviations), then practise daily. Check the letter count against the obvious answer — a mismatch is the fastest tell that a word is being used cryptically.
