Cryptic crosswords run on hidden synonyms. Solvers quickly learn that "flower" can mean a river (a thing that flows) and that "number" can mean an anaesthetic. The zodiac is one of the richest of these seams: twelve signs, each with a symbol, each of which a setter can swap in to lead you astray. Learn the dozen zodiac words and a whole category of clues suddenly opens up.

The trick works both ways. A setter can clue **ARIES** with "the Ram", or clue **RAM** with "Aries" — the sign and its symbol are interchangeable. Miss the connection and the clue looks impossible; spot it and the answer is instant.

<Callout>
**The core idea:** every zodiac sign has an everyday symbol — a ram, a crab, a set of scales — and setters use that symbol as a synonym. "The Archer" is Sagittarius; "the Scales" is Libra. Learn the pairs and you can read them in either direction.
</Callout>

## The Zodiac's Crossword Aliases

Here are the twelve signs with the words a setter is most likely to use for each. None is exotic — that is exactly why they slip past you in a smooth surface.

![The twelve zodiac signs and their crossword aliases: Aries the Ram, Taurus the Bull, Gemini the Twins, Cancer the Crab, Leo the Lion, Virgo the Maiden, Libra the Scales, Scorpio the Scorpion, Sagittarius the Archer, Capricorn the Goat, Aquarius the Water-bearer, Pisces the Fishes](/images/zodiac-crossword-words.svg)

A few of these are especially common in grids. **LEO** turns up constantly — not just as "the Lion" or "star sign", but as a chunk of letters buried inside longer words. **RAM**, **CRAB**, **GOAT**, **SCALES** and **BULL** are short, useful answers a setter can clue by their sign. And "the Twins" for **GEMINI** or "the Water-bearer" for **AQUARIUS** are classic definition-by-symbol misdirections.

## How a Sign Hides in a Clue

The neatest zodiac clues are double definitions, where the same answer is defined twice — once by its sign meaning and once by its everyday meaning.

![A worked example: "The Scales, or a balance (5)" — "the Scales" defines the sign, "a balance" defines its meaning, and both point to LIBRA](/images/zodiac-clue-example.svg)

Take *"The Scales, or a balance (5)"*. There is no anagram, no hidden word, no indicator at all — just two definitions side by side. "The Scales" is the zodiac sign, and "a balance" is what the word literally means. Both point to **LIBRA**, the Latin for scales and the seventh sign of the zodiac. Recognising that a double definition is in play — and that "the Scales" is doing zodiac duty — is the whole solve.

This is the same skill you build reading our guide to [misleading words](/cryptic-misleading-words): a plain-looking word ("scales", "ram", "bull") is quietly standing in for something else.

## Beyond the Twelve Signs

The zodiac is just the start of the crossword's astrological vocabulary. Setters reach constantly for the wider night sky:

- **Planets as gods.** MARS is the god of war; VENUS is love or a goddess; MERCURY is a messenger — or the metal. Setters flip between the planet and the myth.
- **STAR itself.** "Star" can mean a celebrity, an asterisk, or lead a clue toward a specific answer — and "sign" often flags the zodiac.
- **Astrological odds and ends.** "House", "rising", "the heavens" and "chart" all carry astrological second meanings a setter can exploit.

Because these words are so ordinary, they make ideal fodder. The more of them you recognise on sight, the fewer clues can ambush you — and the faster you spot which everyday word is secretly pulling double duty. For most solvers, the zodiac is purely raw material — a bag of handy synonyms. But if learning the signs sparks a genuine curiosity about the astrology behind them, that is a rabbit hole worth its own afternoon.
