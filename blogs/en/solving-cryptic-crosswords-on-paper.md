Plenty of cryptic crossword solvers do everything on screen now — and yet a huge number of us still reach for a printout and a pen. It is not just nostalgia. Paper changes how you solve: it slows you down in a good way, gives you room to think with your hands, and removes the temptation to peek at a hint the moment a clue gets hard.

If you have never solved a cryptic on paper, or you struggle to get a clean copy to print, this is a short, practical guide to doing it well — from the marginal scribbles that actually crack anagrams to turning any puzzle into a tidy, printable file.

<Callout>

</Callout>

## Why Solve on Paper

Screens are wonderful for the daily puzzle, but paper has real, specific advantages once the clues get tricky.

![Solving on paper: fewer distractions and room to scribble — the margin becomes your workspace, with a printed grid and a pen](/images/cryptics-on-paper-cover.svg)

- **Fewer distractions.** No notifications, no tabs, no auto-checking. Just you and the grid, which is exactly the focused state cryptics reward.
- **Room to think with your hands.** You can jot the letters of an anagram, sketch a container, or note a half-solved clue in the margin — and the physical act of writing often shakes the answer loose.
- **No temptation to cheat.** On screen, the hint button is one tap away. On paper you have to sit with a clue, and sitting with it is how you improve.
- **It is portable and kind on the eyes.** A folded printout goes anywhere, works on a plane, and never runs out of battery.

## The Margin Is Your Workspace

The single biggest reason paper helps is scratch space. Anagrams in particular become far easier the moment you stop trying to rearrange letters in your head and simply write them out.

![Using the margin as scratch space: the six letters of "garden" written out jumbled, then rearranged into the answer DANGER](/images/paper-margin-scratch.svg)

Take a clue like *"Garden ruined? That's a risk (6)"*. "Ruined" flags an anagram and "risk" is the definition, so you need to rearrange the letters of GARDEN. In your head that is hard. On paper, you write the six letters out in a loose ring, cross each one off as you place it, and DANGER falls out in seconds.

The same trick works for almost every device: write the fodder down, tick letters off as you use them, and let the page hold the information your working memory would otherwise drop. If you get stuck, our [anagram solver](/anagram-solver) can confirm what the letters can make — but try the margin first, because doing it by hand is what builds the skill.

## Getting a Puzzle Onto Paper

Here is the practical bit. Getting a clean, print-ready copy is easy once you know the options.

![Get any puzzle print-ready: a screen grab or web page converts to a clean A4 PDF, which then prints onto paper](/images/paper-print-ready.svg)

- **Print straight from the browser.** Most puzzle pages print well directly. Use your browser's print dialog and choose "Save as PDF" if you want a copy to keep or send.
- **Convert a screenshot or image.** If all you have is a photo or a screen grab of a grid, it will not print cleanly as-is. A file converter like [filevo](https://filevo.app/) turns an image, screenshot, or awkward web page into a tidy PDF sized for A4 or Letter — and can go the other way too, converting a PDF back to an image or merging several puzzles into one file to print in a single batch.
- **Keep a folder of PDFs.** Once your puzzles are PDFs, they are easy to store, reprint, and take with you. Building a small archive of favourites means you always have something to solve offline.

## Print Settings That Actually Matter

A few small choices make the difference between a crisp grid and a cramped, greyish mess.

- **Fit to page.** Turn on "shrink/fit to printable area" so the grid and clues do not get clipped at the edges.
- **Print in black and white.** Cryptic grids need no colour. Greyscale is sharper and saves ink.
- **Give yourself margins.** Do not print edge to edge — you want that white border for your scratch work.
- **Bump the scale if your eyes prefer it.** Printing a single puzzle slightly enlarged makes the grid roomier and the clues easier to read.

## Bring It Back to the Solving

Paper is a tool, not a rule — plenty of brilliant solvers never print a thing. But if you have been struggling to make progress on screen, a printout and a pen are worth trying for a week. The extra room to think, and the quiet of no hint button, tend to lift everyone's solving.
