Minute Cryptic does one thing beautifully: a single clue, a minute or two of head-scratching, and a satisfying click when the wordplay falls into place. Then it is over until tomorrow.

If that leaves you wanting more, you are not alone. The good news is that the daily puzzle world is full of games that exercise the same muscles, from wordplay and anagrams to lateral thinking and vocabulary. We publish daily hints and answers for most of these games on this site, so this list draws on close, day-to-day familiarity with how each one works rather than on app-store descriptions.

**What makes a game "like Minute Cryptic"?** A game is like Minute Cryptic when it is short, released daily and built on wordplay: reading words in more than one way, rearranging letters or spotting a hidden link. The closest matches also reward the "aha" moment more than raw speed or trivia.

## 1. NYT Connections: Best for Wordplay Lovers

**What it is:** sixteen words, four hidden groups of four. You select four words you think belong together, and you are allowed four mistakes. Groups are colour-coded by difficulty, from yellow to purple.

**Why minute cryptic fans love it:** the purple group is essentially a cryptic clue without an indicator. Our analysis of 660 Connections puzzles found that **77% of purple groups rely on wordplay** such as fill-in-the-blank phrases, hidden words and homophones. If you enjoy spotting that "African Alps" hides CANAL, you will enjoy spotting that MOHAWK hides HAWK.

**Time:** 3–10 minutes.

**Tip:** read all sixteen words aloud before grouping anything. It catches homophone groups immediately, and it slows you down enough to notice odd spellings that signal a hidden-word group.

**On our site:** [today's Connections hints](/connections-hint-today) reveal one group at a time, our [unlimited Connections-style game](/connections-game) lets you keep playing, and our [purple group study](/blog/nyt-connections-purple-category) explains the six tricks behind the hardest group.

## 2. NYT Strands: Best for Theme Spotters

**What it is:** a six-by-eight grid of letters hiding a set of theme words and one special "spangram" that spans the board and names the theme. A short, punning clue is your only hint.

**Why minute cryptic fans love it:** the theme clue is almost always wordplay. "Re-fresh-ing" pointed to LAUNDRY DAY; "Who ya gonna call?" pointed to EXTERMINATOR. Reading the clue the wrong way is the whole game.

**Time:** 5–10 minutes.

**Tip:** remember that the theme words and spangram always use all 48 letters. Subtract each word you find from 48 to see how much is left.

**On our site:** [today's Strands hints](/strands-hint-today), an [unlimited Strands-style game](/strands-game) and our [study of 373 spangrams](/blog/strands-spangram-patterns), which found that every spangram of six or seven letters ran horizontally.

## 3. NYT Spelling Bee: Best for Vocabulary Builders

**What it is:** seven letters in a honeycomb. You make as many words of four or more letters as you can, always using the centre letter, and climb ranks up to Genius and Queen Bee.

**Why minute cryptic fans love it:** building words from parts is exactly how charade clues work. Turning TINT into TINTED and TINTING uses the same instinct as building SEASON from SEA and SON.

**Time:** 10–30 minutes, often in short bursts across the day.

**Tip:** after a quick sweep of four-letter words, move on to longer ones. Five- and six-letter words supply more than half of all the points.

**On our site:** [today's Spelling Bee answers](/spelling-bee-answers-today) and our [Spelling Bee statistics](/blog/spelling-bee-statistics), which found that four-letter words are a third of all answers but only 7% of the points.

## 4. Wordle: Best Quick Warm-Up

**What it is:** guess a five-letter word in six tries. After each guess, the letters turn green, yellow or grey to show which are right, misplaced or absent.

**Why minute cryptic fans like it:** Wordle is about deduction rather than wordplay, so it exercises a different muscle. It is also short and daily, which makes it a natural companion to a single cryptic clue.

**Time:** 2–5 minutes.

**On our site:** [today's Wordle answer and hints](/wordle-answer-today), a [Wordle solver](/wordle-solver) and [unlimited Wordle](/wordle-unlimited).

## 5. NYT Letter Boxed: Best for Anagram Fans

**What it is:** twelve letters arranged three to a side around a square. You build words by moving between sides, and each word must start with the last letter of the previous one. The goal is to use every letter, ideally in as few words as possible.

**Why minute cryptic fans love it:** it rewards seeing the letters you have rather than the words you expect, which is the anagram solver's core skill.

**Time:** 5–15 minutes.

**On our site:** [today's Letter Boxed answers](/letter-boxed-answers-today) and the full [Letter Boxed archive](/letter-boxed-answers).

## 6. The NYT Mini Crossword: Best Gateway to Full Crosswords

**What it is:** a small crossword, usually five squares by five, with plain definition clues. Most people finish it in a couple of minutes.

**Why minute cryptic fans like it:** the Mini trains quick recall of synonyms, which is exactly what you need for the definition half of every cryptic clue. It is also a gentle introduction to how crossings help you in a full grid.

**Time:** 1–5 minutes.

## 7. Quordle: Best for Wordle Players Who Want More

**What it is:** four Wordle puzzles at once, solved with the same guesses, in nine tries.

**Why minute cryptic fans like it:** like Wordle, it is a deduction game rather than a wordplay game, but juggling four boards at once is a satisfying step up in difficulty.

**Time:** 5–10 minutes.

**On our site:** play [Quordle](/quordle) directly.

## 8. LinkedIn Pinpoint: Best One-Minute Brain Teaser

**What it is:** words are revealed one at a time, up to five, and you try to guess the category that links them using as few clues as possible.

**Why minute cryptic fans like it:** it is the closest thing to a single clue in the LinkedIn games line-up. Like a cryptic definition, the first word often points you in the wrong direction.

**Time:** 1–3 minutes.

**On our site:** [today's Pinpoint answer](/linkedin-pinpoint-answer).

## 9. LinkedIn Crossclimb: Best Mix of Trivia and Wordplay

**What it is:** a word ladder. You solve a set of clues, arrange the answers so that each differs from the next by one letter, and then solve the words at the top and bottom of the ladder.

**Why minute cryptic fans like it:** changing one letter at a time is the same move as a deletion or letter-change clue, and the trivia clues add variety.

**Time:** 3–8 minutes.

**On our site:** [today's Crossclimb answer](/linkedin-crossclimb-answer).

## 10. Newspaper Quick Cryptics: Best Next Step Up

**What it is:** a full cryptic crossword, but smaller and gentler than a newspaper's main cryptic. The Times and The Guardian both publish quick cryptics, using simpler wordplay and more familiar answers.

**Why minute cryptic fans love it:** this is the natural destination. Every clue works exactly like a minute cryptic, and crossing letters help you with the hard ones.

**Time:** 15–30 minutes for most solvers at first.

**How to prepare:** our [30-day plan to get better at cryptic crosswords](/blog/get-better-at-cryptic-crosswords) builds up to a quick cryptic in week four, and our guide to [cryptic vs regular crosswords](/blog/cryptic-vs-regular-crossword) explains what changes when you move to a full grid.

## Honourable Mentions: Logic Puzzles

Not every daily puzzle is about words. If you enjoy the deduction side of cryptics, the careful step-by-step reasoning that confirms an answer, these logic games are worth a look. None of them involve wordplay, so they sit outside our top ten, but many minute cryptic fans play them alongside.

- **NYT Pips** asks you to place dominoes on a grid so that coloured regions meet conditions such as a target sum. Our [Pips answers](/pips-answers) explain each day's solution.
- **LinkedIn Queens** is a placement puzzle: one queen in every row, column and coloured region, with no two queens touching. See [today's Queens answer](/linkedin-queens-answer).
- **LinkedIn Tango** fills a grid with two symbols under balance and adjacency rules. See [today's Tango answer](/linkedin-tango-answer).
- **LinkedIn Zip** asks you to draw a single path through numbered checkpoints that covers every square. See [today's Zip answer](/linkedin-zip-answer).

They make a good contrast with a cryptic clue: pure logic after pure wordplay.

## Why Short Daily Puzzles Work So Well

There is a reason so many of the games on this list share the same shape: one puzzle a day, a few minutes long, the same for everyone.

**The daily limit protects the habit.** Because you cannot binge, you come back tomorrow. A few minutes every day adds up to far more practice than an occasional long session.

**Small puzzles teach faster.** A single cryptic clue or a single Connections board gives you one clear lesson at a time. When you get it wrong, the explanation is short enough to absorb.

**Shared puzzles are social.** Everyone gets the same Wordle, the same Connections board and the same daily cryptic clue, which is why people compare results with friends and family.

That is also why the best way to improve at cryptics is a little every day rather than a whole newspaper grid once a month.

## Build a 15-Minute Daily Puzzle Routine

Many solvers settle into a short routine that mixes skills. Here is one that takes about fifteen minutes and exercises wordplay, vocabulary and deduction in turn.

![A 15-minute daily puzzle routine: 2 minutes for a minute cryptic clue, 5 minutes for Connections, 5 minutes for Strands and 3 minutes for Wordle, covering wordplay, lateral thinking, theme spotting and deduction](/images/blog/games-like-minute-cryptic/daily-routine.webp)

1. **A minute cryptic clue (about 2 minutes).** Start with the hardest thinking while your mind is fresh.
2. **Connections (about 5 minutes).** Wordplay again, but across sixteen words.
3. **Strands (about 5 minutes).** Theme spotting and searching.
4. **Wordle (about 3 minutes).** Finish with pure deduction.

On days with more time, add the Spelling Bee in short bursts throughout the day.

## What to Look for in a Daily Word Game

If you are trying a game not on this list, these questions will tell you quickly whether it will suit a minute cryptic fan:

- **Is there one puzzle a day?** Daily release builds a habit and a shared experience.
- **Does it reward reading words two ways?** That is the heart of cryptic solving.
- **Can you finish in under fifteen minutes?** Short puzzles are easier to keep up.
- **Does it explain itself afterwards?** Learning why an answer works is what makes you better.
- **Is there a hint system?** Good hints let you keep going without giving the answer away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What games are similar to Minute Cryptic?

The closest matches are NYT Connections, whose hardest purple group relies on wordplay 77% of the time in our analysis, NYT Strands, whose theme clues are puns, and newspaper quick cryptics such as those in The Times and The Guardian. Spelling Bee and Letter Boxed are good choices if you enjoy building words.

### Is there a free alternative to Minute Cryptic?

Yes. Our [daily minute cryptic](/minute-cryptic-today) is free, with one clue a day, four levels of hints and a full explanation, plus an archive of past clues and an unlimited practice mode. Many of the other games on this list, including Wordle, Connections and Strands, are also free to play daily.

### What should I play after Minute Cryptic to get better at cryptic crosswords?

Move to a newspaper quick cryptic, which uses the same clue types in a small grid with simpler wordplay. Before that, practising one clue type at a time helps: our 30-day plan works through each device and builds up to a quick cryptic in its fourth week.

### Is Connections a cryptic puzzle?

Not exactly, but its purple group comes close. In our analysis of 660 puzzles, 77% of purple groups relied on wordplay such as fill-in-the-blank phrases, hidden words and homophones, the same tricks cryptic clues use. The difference is that a cryptic clue tells you which trick to use.

### Which daily word game is the hardest?

Of the games on this list, newspaper quick cryptics are usually the most demanding for newcomers, because every clue uses wordplay and the grid is larger. Among the shorter games, many players find the Spelling Bee's Queen Bee rank and Connections' purple group the toughest targets.

### How long should a daily puzzle routine take?

Fifteen minutes is plenty for most people: a minute cryptic clue, Connections, Strands and Wordle fit comfortably in that time. Short, regular sessions build skills faster than occasional long ones, and they are easier to keep up.
