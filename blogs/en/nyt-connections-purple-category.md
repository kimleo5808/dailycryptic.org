Most Connections streaks don't end on yellow. They end on purple, when the last four words on the board refuse to share anything you can name.

We wanted to know whether purple is truly unpredictable or whether it follows patterns, so we went through the archive. We sorted every group from **660 NYT Connections puzzles**, from 28 December 2024 to 29 October 2026 — 2,640 groups in all — by the kind of link it uses.

**What is the purple category in Connections?** The purple category is the hardest of the four colour-coded groups in NYT Connections. Yellow is the most straightforward, followed by green and blue. Purple groups usually link their words through wordplay, such as hidden words, homophones or fill-in-the-blank phrases, rather than through meaning.

The short version: purple is not random. It draws on a small toolkit of tricks, and once you know them, you can test for each one in a few seconds.

<Callout>
**Key findings from 660 puzzles**

- **77% of purple groups** (507 of 660) depend on wordplay. Only **1.7%** of yellow groups do.
- **Fill in the blank** is the single biggest trick, behind **31%** of all purple groups.
- Words hidden at the **start or end** of longer words come second, at **21%**.
- Purple is getting trickier: **72%** wordplay in 2025, **83%** in 2026.
- **BELL** is the ultimate purple word: 7 of its 11 appearances were in the purple group.
</Callout>

## How We Did This

Every Connections puzzle reveals its four category names once it is solved, such as "___ BAR" or "HOMOPHONES OF PATHS". Those labels are written by the puzzle's editor, so they tell you exactly what kind of link the group uses.

We classified all 2,640 category names into ten families using a set of rules, then checked samples from every family by hand. A label like "WORDS BEFORE 'FORCE'" counts as fill-in-the-blank. "TAXONOMIC RANKS PLUS A LETTER" counts as a letter change. "SPREADSHEET FUNCTIONS" counts as a plain meaning category.

Eight of the families are **wordplay**, meaning the link depends on how the words are spelled, sounded or built rather than what they mean. Two are **meaning** families: plain categories and "shared property" groups such as "THINGS WITH FACES".

<Callout>
**Caveats:** a few labels sit on the border between two families, and our rules assign each label to exactly one. We estimate that moves any single percentage by a point or two at most. The archive covers 660 consecutive puzzles, not the whole history of the game. dailycryptic.org is an independent puzzle site and is not affiliated with The New York Times.
</Callout>

## Trick 1: Fill in the Blank (31%)

Fill-in-the-blank is the workhorse of the purple group. Each word pairs with one hidden word to make a familiar phrase or compound, and the category name shows where the hidden word goes.

Recent examples from the archive:

- **BELL ___** (Connections #1234, 27 October 2026): PEPPER, JAR, TOWER, BOTTOMS — bell pepper, bell jar, bell tower, bell-bottoms.
- **___ BAR** (#1231, 24 October 2026): SALAD, CANDY, SPACE, SPORTS — salad bar, candy bar, space bar, sports bar.
- **___ TAP** (#1230, 23 October 2026): LOVE, SPINAL, PHONE, DOUBLE — love tap, spinal tap, phone tap, double tap.

The hidden word can sit on either side. In our archive, the four words went **before** the fixed word 107 times and **after** it 97 times, so it is close to an even split. Always test both directions.

**How to spot it:** pick a word that seems out of place and say it with common partners in front and behind: *bell ___, ___ bell, bar ___, ___ bar*. When one partner suddenly works for three or four words on the board, you have found the group.

💡 **Pro tip:** fill-in-the-blank groups often hide in plain sight because each word is perfectly ordinary. JAR, TOWER and PEPPER look like a random shopping list until you put BELL in front of them.

## Trick 2: Hidden Starts and Ends (21%)

The second most common purple trick hides a word inside each answer, at the very start or the very end. The category name tells you what the hidden words have in common.

- **STARTS OF BEDDING** (#1229, 22 October 2026): COMFORT, BLANK, PILL, SHE — the first parts of comforter, blanket, pillow and sheet.
- **ENDING IN BIRDS** (#1226, 19 October 2026): MOHAWK, BALLOON, ALIBIS, ESCROW — hawk, loon, ibis, crow.
- **STARTING WITH WORDS FOR "STYLISH"** (#1223, 16 October 2026): HIPPO, CHICKEN, COOLIDGE, SMARTIES — hip, chic, cool, smart.

Starts were slightly more common than ends: 78 groups against 61.

**How to spot it:** look for words that feel slightly wrong for any obvious category, especially proper names (COOLIDGE) and long words (ESCROW). Read their first three to five letters, then their last three to five. Hidden starts and ends are usually short, common words.

📝 **Note:** this is exactly how hidden-word clues work in cryptic crosswords, where the answer sits inside a longer phrase. If you enjoy spotting MOHAWK's hawk, you will enjoy [hidden word clues](/cryptic-clue-types/hidden-word).

## Trick 3: Add, Drop or Change a Letter (10%)

Letter-change groups take a set of ordinary words and alter one letter. The result is often a real word that looks innocent, which is what makes these groups so slippery.

- **HORROR MOVIES WITH LAST LETTER CHANGED** (#1233, 26 October 2026): HALLOWEEK, JAWN, PSYCHS, HOSTED — Halloween, Jaws, Psycho, Hostel.
- **TAXONOMIC RANKS PLUS A LETTER** (#1202, 25 September 2026): GENIUS, BORDER, CLASSY, FOAMILY — genus, order, class, family.

Adding a letter was the most common version (34 groups), followed by removing one (19) and changing one (12).

**How to spot it:** watch for words that are almost words, such as FOAMILY or HALLOWEEK. One invented spelling on the board is a strong signal that the whole group has been tampered with. Then look for real words that would become a set if you removed or changed one letter.

## Trick 4: Sound-Alikes (7%)

Sound groups only make sense out loud. Almost all of them are homophones (39 of 49), with a handful of rhymes and other sound games.

- **HOMOPHONES OF PATHS** (#1235, 28 October 2026): ROUT, COARSE, WEIGH, RODE — route, course, way, road.
- **HOMOPHONES OF DISNEY CHARACTERS** (#1227, 20 October 2026): DOCK, BELL, MINI, ARIAL — Doc, Belle, Minnie, Ariel.

**How to spot it:** read every word on the board aloud, quickly, before you try to group anything. Unusual spellings such as ARIAL or COARSE are the giveaway. Your eye trusts the spelling, but your ear hears the hidden word.

📖 **Further reading:** cryptic crosswords have a whole clue type built on this trick. See our guide to [solving homophone clues](/blog/how-to-solve-homophone-clues).

## Trick 5: One Word, Several Meanings (5%)

This family flips the usual logic. Instead of four words with one meaning, you get four meanings of one hidden word.

- **WHAT "STOCK" MIGHT REFER TO** (#1151, 5 August 2026): CATTLE, INVESTMENT, BROTH, MERCHANDISE.
- **WHAT "W" MIGHT STAND FOR** (#1120, 5 July 2026): WIN, WEST, WITH, TUNGSTEN.

**How to spot it:** when four words seem to come from completely different worlds, such as farming, finance, cooking and retail, ask whether one short word could mean all of them. The more unrelated the words look, the more likely this trick is.

## Trick 6: Anagrams, Hidden Words and Spelling Games (2.6%)

The rarest tricks appear only a few times a year, which makes them hard to anticipate. Together, anagrams, reversals, hidden words and spelling games made up 17 purple groups in our archive.

- **ANAGRAMS** (#1080, 26 May 2026): TINSEL, SILENT, LISTEN, ENLIST — the same six letters rearranged.
- **BACKWARDS ANIMALS** (#899, 26 November 2025): MAR, GOD, FLOW, TAB — ram, dog, wolf and bat, reversed.
- **PHRASES HIDING GREEK LETTERS** (#1206, 29 September 2026): MODEL TANK, FREEZE TAG, ORAL PHASE, HOME GAME — mo**DEL TA**nk, free**ZE TA**g, or**AL PHA**se, h**OME GA**me.

**How to spot it:** when every other approach fails, write the words down and look at the letters rather than the words. Can they be rearranged? Read backwards? Is a short word straddling two longer ones?

These are, again, the classic tools of cryptic crosswords. The Greek-letter group is a perfect example of a cryptic hidden word, and TINSEL/SILENT/LISTEN is a textbook [anagram](/cryptic-clue-types/anagram).

## When Purple Is Just Hard Knowledge (23%)

Not every purple group is wordplay. About one in four is a meaning group that happens to be obscure, specialised or deliberately broad.

- **THINGS THAT CAN BE SNAPPED** (#1232, 25 October 2026): GLOW STICK, FOOTBALL, FINGERS, RUBBER BAND.
- **SPREADSHEET FUNCTIONS** (#1180, 3 September 2026): MAX, AVERAGE, SUM, COUNT.
- **"COOL" THINGS, IN EXPRESSIONS** (#1203, 26 September 2026): BEANS, CAT, CUCUMBER, CUSTOMER.

"Shared property" groups (32 of them) are the trickiest of these. The words share a feature or an action, such as "can be snapped" or "they have faces", rather than belonging to one category. They make up 4.8% of purple groups, but they also turn up in green and blue.

**How to spot it:** if no wordplay test works, ask what the leftover words can *do* or what they *have*, rather than what they *are*.

## Purple Has Got Trickier

The share of wordplay in purple groups has risen. Across 2025 it was 71.9%. So far in 2026 it is 82.9%.

![Column chart of the share of purple groups that use wordplay by half-year: 71.1% in the first half of 2025, 72.7% in the second half of 2025, 82.8% in the first half of 2026 and 83.2% in the second half of 2026 to date](/images/blog/nyt-connections-purple-category/trend.webp)

| Period | Purple groups | Wordplay | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan–Jun 2025 | 180 | 128 | 71.1% |
| Jul–Dec 2025 | 183 | 133 | 72.7% |
| Jan–Jun 2026 | 174 | 144 | 82.8% |
| Jul–29 Oct 2026 | 119 | 99 | 83.2% |

The four puzzles from the last days of December 2024 are left out of this table.

This is not noise. Comparing 2025 with 2026, the difference is statistically significant (a two-proportion test gives z = 3.33, well beyond the usual 1.96 threshold).

In practical terms, a 2026 purple group is wordplay more than four times out of five. If you are still approaching purple as "the hardest meaning category", you are using a strategy built for a puzzle that no longer exists.

## Does the Day of the Week Matter?

The NYT crossword famously gets harder from Monday to Saturday. Connections has no published difficulty schedule, so we checked whether purple behaves differently on different days.

| Day | Purple groups | Wordplay share |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 94 | 79.8% |
| Tuesday | 95 | 74.7% |
| Wednesday | 94 | 84.0% |
| Thursday | 95 | 68.4% |
| Friday | 94 | 86.2% |
| Saturday | 93 | 74.2% |
| Sunday | 95 | 70.5% |

There is no steady climb through the week. Friday and Wednesday purples leaned hardest on wordplay, and Thursday the least. The spread is just large enough to pass a standard significance test (chi-square 14.3 against a 5% threshold of 12.6), but with only about 94 puzzles per day, we would treat it as a mild tendency rather than a rule.

## The Words That Keep Coming Back

Across 660 puzzles there were 10,560 word slots, filled by **6,511 different words**. Most words appear once and never return: **70%** of them were used a single time.

A small set keeps coming back, and those repeat visitors are worth knowing because they are favourite red herrings.

BALL is the most reused word, but it is usually a yellow word (7 of its 14 appearances). **BELL** is the opposite. It lands in purple almost two-thirds of the time, nearly always as part of a fill-in-the-blank or sound group. Among words used at least six times, others that lean purple include GOOSE, BOX, CLOCK and FISH (4 of 6 each) and PEPPER (4 of 7).

💡 **Pro tip:** when BELL, BAR or PEPPER appears on your board, test fill-in-the-blank phrases before anything else.

## A Four-Step Routine for Finding Purple

The data points to a simple routine. It takes under a minute and covers the tricks behind roughly seven in ten purple groups.

### Step 1: Read the board aloud

This catches sound-alikes immediately, and it costs nothing. Any word that sounds like a different word goes on your list.

### Step 2: Hunt for odd spellings and proper names

Invented words (FOAMILY), names (COOLIDGE) and slightly strange choices (ESCROW) are rarely on the board by accident. They point to letter changes and hidden starts or ends.

### Step 3: Try common partners front and back

Take two or three suspicious words and test *___ word* and *word ___* with everyday partners. This covers fill-in-the-blank, the biggest purple family of all.

### Step 4: Check the first and last letters

Look at the first and last three to five letters of every remaining word. Hidden starts and ends are the second-biggest family.

Then solve yellow and green to shrink the board. Even when you cannot see purple, removing eight words often reveals it by elimination.

**Should you go for purple first?** Only if one of the four tests above lights up clearly. Otherwise, clear the meaning groups first. With 97% of yellow and green groups being meaning groups rather than wordplay, they are where your early, low-risk progress comes from.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the purple category in NYT Connections?

The purple category is the hardest of the four groups in NYT Connections. In our analysis of 660 puzzles, 77% of purple groups relied on wordplay rather than meaning: fill-in-the-blank phrases, words hidden at the start or end of longer words, letter changes, homophones and similar tricks.

### Why is purple the hardest Connections group?

Purple is hard because it usually ignores what the words mean. A purple group often links words by spelling or sound, as in "HOMOPHONES OF PATHS" (ROUT, COARSE, WEIGH, RODE). Your brain naturally sorts words by meaning, so purple works against your instincts.

### What are the most common purple tricks in Connections?

Fill in the blank is the most common, behind 31% of purple groups in our archive, followed by words hidden at the start or end of longer words (21%), letter changes (10%) and homophones (7%). Together those four account for about seven in ten purple groups.

### Should I solve the purple group first or last?

Solve it first only when a wordplay test, such as reading the words aloud or trying fill-in-the-blank partners, clearly lights up four words. Otherwise solve yellow and green first. In our data, 97% of yellow and green groups are meaning groups rather than wordplay, so they are the safest early progress.

### Has Connections purple got harder?

It has become more trick-based. In our archive, 71.9% of purple groups used wordplay in 2025, compared with 82.9% in 2026 up to 29 October. That rise is statistically significant, so expect wordplay from almost every purple group now.

### What word appears most often in Connections?

In the 660 puzzles we analysed (December 2024 to October 2026), BALL appeared most often, 14 times, followed by TIME and RING with 12 each. BELL is the word most likely to be purple: 7 of its 11 appearances were in the purple group.
