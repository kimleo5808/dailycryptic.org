Most people who give up on cryptic crosswords do not give up because they are bad at them. They give up because they practise at random: a whole newspaper grid one day, nothing for a week, then a clue that seems impossible and a decision that cryptics "are not for them".

Getting better is mostly a matter of order. Learn the devices one at a time, practise each on clues that are easy enough to finish, and add difficulty only when the basics are automatic. This plan does exactly that in **30 days of about 15 minutes each**.

**How do you get better at cryptic crosswords?** You get better at cryptic crosswords by learning the eight main wordplay devices one at a time, practising each on easy clues, reading the explanation for every clue you attempt, and keeping a personal list of indicator words. Short daily practice beats occasional long sessions.

**What you'll need:**

- About 15 minutes a day
- A notebook or a notes app for your indicator list and mistakes log
- Our free [daily minute cryptic](/minute-cryptic-today) plus the archives, sorted by [easy](/minute-cryptic/easy), [medium](/minute-cryptic/medium) and [hard](/minute-cryptic/hard)
- Our guides to each [cryptic clue type](/cryptic-clue-types)

As of October 2026 our archive holds 257 published clues (123 easy, 97 medium and 37 hard), each with hints and an explanation, which is more than enough material for the whole plan.

## How the Plan Works

The plan has four weeks and a two-day finale. Each week has a single focus, and every day has one small task.

![A 30-day cryptic crossword practice calendar in four colour-coded weeks: week 1 learns six core devices, week 2 covers containers, deletions, abbreviations and indicators, week 3 builds skills with drills and medium clues, week 4 tackles hard clues and full puzzles, and days 29 and 30 are a mock test and benchmark](/images/blog/get-better-at-cryptic-crosswords/calendar.webp)

| Week | Focus | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | The six simplest devices | Easy |
| Week 2 | Containers, deletions and the vocabulary | Easy to medium |
| Week 3 | Drills, speed and independence | Medium |
| Week 4 | Hard clues and full puzzles | Medium to hard |

Three rules apply every day:

1. **Always read the explanation**, even when you solve the clue.
2. **Write down any indicator that surprised you.** This list becomes your most valuable tool.
3. **Log your mistakes** in one line each: the clue, what you missed, and why.

## Five Habits That Make the Plan Work

The daily tasks matter, but these habits matter more. They are what turn 15 minutes a day into steady progress.

### 1. Read the explanation every time

The explanation is where the learning happens. Read it on days you solve the clue as well as days you do not, and check that your reasoning matches. A right answer for a wrong reason is a mistake waiting to happen.

### 2. Work from both ends

Before you try any wordplay, test the first and last words as definitions. This single habit removes half the confusion of a new clue.

### 3. Say the clue out loud

Reading aloud breaks the spell of the surface story and catches homophones immediately. It also slows you down just enough to notice small words such as "a", "in" and "about".

### 4. Write things down

Write anagram fodder in a circle, draw containers as one word wrapped around another, and keep your indicator list in one place. Puzzles that seem impossible in your head often fall apart on paper.

### 5. Stop after 15 minutes

Short sessions keep the habit sustainable. If a clue defeats you, use the hints, read the explanation, log the mistake and stop. Tomorrow's clue will teach you more than another twenty minutes of frustration.

## Week 1: Learn the Core Devices

Week 1 introduces one device a day, starting with the most approachable. Each day, read the guide for that clue type, then solve three easy clues of the same type from the archive.

| Day | Focus | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How a cryptic clue works | Read our [beginner's guide](/cryptic-crossword-for-beginners), then attempt today's clue |
| 2 | [Double definitions](/cryptic-clue-types/double-definition) | Solve 3 easy double definitions |
| 3 | [Hidden words](/cryptic-clue-types/hidden-word) | Solve 3 easy hidden-word clues |
| 4 | [Anagrams](/cryptic-clue-types/anagram) | Solve 3 easy anagrams; write the fodder letters in a circle |
| 5 | [Charades](/cryptic-clue-types/charade) | Solve 3 easy charades |
| 6 | [Reversals](/cryptic-clue-types/reversal) | Solve 3 easy reversals |
| 7 | [Homophones](/cryptic-clue-types/homophone) | Solve 3 easy homophones, then redo any clue you missed this week |

The order is deliberate. Double definitions and hidden words teach you to find the definition, the single most important skill. Anagrams and charades teach you to build answers from pieces. Reversals and homophones round out the toolkit with two devices that are easy to spot once you know the indicators.

💡 **Pro tip:** on Day 1, do not worry about solving the clue. The goal is to understand what a clue *is*: a definition at one end, wordplay in the rest. If you can point at both halves after reading the explanation, Day 1 is a success.

### What a good Week 1 looks like

By Day 7, you should be able to look at any easy clue and say which end is the definition, even if you cannot solve it yet. That is the foundation for everything that follows.

## Week 2: The Harder Devices and the Vocabulary

Week 2 adds the two devices that trouble beginners most, then builds the vocabulary that makes everything faster.

| Day | Focus | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | [Containers](/cryptic-clue-types/container) | Solve 3 container clues; draw the outer word around the inner one |
| 9 | [Deletions](/cryptic-clue-types/deletion) | Solve 3 deletion clues; learn "head", "tail" and "heart" |
| 10 | Abbreviations | Learn the 48 on our [abbreviations cheat sheet](/blog/cryptic-crossword-abbreviations) |
| 11 | Indicators | Start your indicator list using our [indicator guide](/cryptic-indicators) |
| 12 | Misdirection | Read about [misleading words](/cryptic-misleading-words) and [surface reading](/blog/surface-reading-cryptic-clues) |
| 13 | Mixed easy set | Solve 5 mixed easy clues using only the first hint |
| 14 | Review | Redo every clue in your mistakes log |

Containers matter more than their numbers suggest. In our own library of 1,004 clues, containers make up 5% of easy clues but 16% of hard clues. Learning them now pays off in Week 4.

Abbreviations are the other big unlock. Many "impossible" clues turn out to hinge on one small word: "nothing" for O, "at home" for IN, "resistance" for R. Day 10 turns those surprises into knowledge. For quick lookups while you solve, keep our A–Z [abbreviations reference](/cryptic-abbreviations) open in another tab.

### Common sticking points in Week 2

Two problems come up again and again in Week 2.

The first is **putting a container together inside out**. "BOTH clutching R" means BOTH goes around R, giving BROTH, not R around BOTH. Draw the outer word with a gap and write the inner piece into the gap.

The second is **deleting the wrong letter**. "Headless" means the first letter goes, "endless" the last, and "heartless" the middle. If your result is not a word, check whether you removed the right end before you abandon the clue.

## Week 3: Drills, Speed and Independence

Week 3 moves you to medium clues and breaks solving into separate skills, so you can practise each one in isolation.

| Day | Focus | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Definition drill | Read 10 clues and only mark the definition; check against the explanations |
| 16 | Indicator drill | Read 10 clues and only underline the indicator |
| 17 | Medium clues | Solve 3 medium clues with at most one hint each |
| 18 | Speed baseline | Time yourself on 5 easy clues and write the total down |
| 19 | Jargon and punctuation | Read our guide to [cryptic crossword jargon](/blog/cryptic-crossword-glossary), especially question and exclamation marks |
| 20 | Setting | Write your first clue using our [clue-writing guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-cryptic-clue) |
| 21 | Review | Solve 3 medium clues, then redo your mistakes log |

The drills on Days 15 and 16 feel strange at first, because you are not trying to solve anything. That is the point. Finding definitions and spotting indicators are the two moves that unlock most clues, and isolating them makes both faster.

Day 20 may seem out of place in a solving plan, but writing even one clue changes how you read clues. Once you have tried to hide a definition at the end of a sentence, you start seeing where other setters have hidden theirs.

### Using the definition drill well

For each clue on Day 15, cover the explanation and write down the word or words you think are the definition. Then check. Most beginners discover a bias: they always choose the first word. In our library, the definition sat at the start of 52% of single-definition clues and at the end of 48%, so an always-first habit is wrong almost half the time.

### Using the indicator drill well

On Day 16, underline the indicator in each clue and write the device beside it: anagram, reversal, container and so on. Some clues have no indicator at all, such as double definitions, so mark those too. When you check the explanations, add every indicator you missed to your list. Indicators are a closed vocabulary: the same few hundred words appear again and again. In our own explanations, "found in" signalled a hidden word 29 times and "we hear" signalled a homophone 29 times. The sooner you see these words as instructions rather than ordinary English, the faster every clue becomes.

## Week 4: Hard Clues and Full Puzzles

Week 4 raises the difficulty and introduces the two experiences beyond single clues: rarer clue types and full grids.

| Day | Focus | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Hard containers | Solve 3 hard container clues |
| 23 | Hard mixed set | Solve 3 hard clues of any type |
| 24 | Rarer clue types | Read about [&lit clues and cryptic definitions](/blog/and-lit-clues) and try the practice set |
| 25 | A full grid | Try a newspaper quick cryptic; aim for half the clues, not all |
| 26 | Speed check | Time yourself on 5 easy clues and compare with Day 18 |
| 27 | Setting for others | Write 3 clues for a friend or family member |
| 28 | Weakest type | Find your weakest clue type in your log and solve 5 of it |

A full grid on Day 25 is a different experience from single clues. Crossing letters help, but there are also many more clues to sort through. Do not expect to finish your first one. Solving half of a quick cryptic after four weeks is a genuine achievement.

### How to approach your first full grid

1. **Read every clue once, quickly.** Do not stop to solve; just look for easy targets.
2. **Start with what you know best.** Hidden words and anagrams are the usual footholds, because their indicators are easy to spot.
3. **Use crossing letters.** Each answer you enter gives letters to the clues it crosses. A clue that looked impossible with an empty row may fall with two letters in place.
4. **Skip freely.** In a full grid there is no need to solve in order. Come back to hard clues when the grid has given you more letters.
5. **Check the solution afterwards.** For every clue you missed, work out the wordplay from the answer. That review is worth as much as the solving.

📝 **Note:** hard clues are not only about obscure words. In our library, the average answer length rises from 4.9 letters for easy clues to 5.8 for hard ones, and hard clues lean much more on containers. Difficulty mostly comes from subtler wordplay.

## Days 29–30: Mock Test and Benchmark

The finale measures how far you have come.

- **Day 29: mock test.** Pick 10 random clues from our [unlimited minute cryptic](/minute-cryptic-unlimited) and solve them with no hints. Mark each one solved, partly solved or missed.
- **Day 30: benchmark.** Solve today's clue, then 4 hard clues. Compare your notes with Day 1, when you were learning what a definition was.

Most solvers are surprised by the comparison. Clues that looked like nonsense four weeks earlier now come apart into recognisable pieces, even when the answer still takes time.

## How to Use Hints During the Plan

Hints are part of the plan, not a failure. Use them in order, and try the clue again after each one. Our daily clue offers four levels:

![The hint ladder: hint 1 shows where the definition is, hint 2 names the type of wordplay, hint 3 gives a nudge on the wordplay itself, and hint 4 reveals the first letter; try the clue again after each step](/images/blog/get-better-at-cryptic-crosswords/hint-ladder.webp)

| Week | Hint budget per clue |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | As many as you need |
| Week 2 | Up to three |
| Week 3 | One, two on review days |
| Week 4 | One for hard clues; none for easy ones |

The budget shrinks as your skills grow. By Week 4, the first hint alone, which tells you where the definition is, is usually enough.

## What to Do After 30 Days

Keep the daily habit, but change the mix:

- **Solve the daily clue every day**, and a few archive clues of your weakest type each week.
- **Move up to full grids.** Try a quick cryptic once or twice a week, then a main daily cryptic when quick ones feel comfortable.
- **Keep writing clues.** Setting is the fastest way to understand how setters think.
- **Revisit hard clues** you missed in Week 4. Many will now fall quickly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to learn cryptic crosswords?

Most people can learn the main devices and solve easy clues within a few weeks of short daily practice. Solving full daily cryptics comfortably usually takes months rather than weeks. This 30-day plan is designed to get you to the point where any clue can be taken apart, even if hard ones still take time.

### How many cryptic clues should I practise a day?

Three to five clues a day is plenty for most learners, as long as you read every explanation. Quality matters more than quantity. One clue understood completely teaches more than ten clues guessed at and forgotten.

### Is it cheating to use an anagram solver?

Not while you are learning, as long as you try first. Attempt the anagram yourself, then use a tool such as our [anagram solver](/anagram-solver) to check or to see what you missed. Avoid using it before you have identified the fodder, because finding the fodder is the skill you are building.

### What if I miss a day?

Just carry on from where you stopped. The plan works because of its order, not its dates. If you miss several days, repeat the last review day before moving on.

### What are the best cryptic crosswords for beginners?

Single-clue daily cryptics and newspaper quick cryptics are the best starting points. Single clues let you learn one device at a time without a grid, and quick cryptics use simpler wordplay and more common answers. Our [daily minute cryptic](/minute-cryptic-today) is a good daily starting point.

### Should I learn the clue types before trying real clues?

Learn one type and practise it straight away, rather than reading about all eight first. That is how Week 1 is organised: read about one device, then solve three real clues of that type the same day. Theory sticks much better when it is used immediately.
